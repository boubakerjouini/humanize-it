## AI Detectors Aren't Magic — They're Pattern Matchers

Most people treat AI detectors like a black box. Text goes in, a verdict comes out, and nobody really knows what happened in between. That's a problem. If you don't know what's being measured, you can't fix it.

Here's the truth: AI detectors don't "understand" your writing. They don't read it the way a human would. They scan for statistical patterns — specific, measurable, predictable signals that separate machine-generated text from human-written text. That's it.

So how do AI detectors work, exactly? They run your text through a gauntlet of pattern checks. Each pattern gets a score. Those scores roll up into a final probability: AI or human. Once you understand the individual patterns, you can address them one by one. No guesswork.

HumanizeIt's detection engine analyzes 40 patterns total. In this article, we're breaking down the 24 most impactful ones — the patterns that move the needle the most. We'll show you what each one measures, why it matters, and how to fix it.

## The Core Concept: Perplexity and Burstiness

Before we get into the specific patterns, you need to understand the two foundational metrics that underpin almost every AI detector on the market.

### Perplexity: How Predictable Is Your Writing?

Perplexity measures how surprising your word choices are. When a language model generates text, it picks the most statistically likely next word at each step. The result? Smooth, predictable sentences where every word feels like the "obvious" choice.

Human writing is different. We pick weird words. We use phrasing that a probability model wouldn't predict. We write things that are technically less "optimal" but more interesting.

Compare these two sentences:

**Low perplexity (AI-like):** "Artificial intelligence has the potential to transform various industries and improve efficiency across multiple sectors."

**High perplexity (human-like):** "AI is going to gut some industries and supercharge others — and honestly, nobody knows which ones yet."

Both say roughly the same thing. But the second one has unexpected word choices ("gut," "supercharge"), an em dash, a personal aside. A detector would score the first as far more likely to be AI-generated.

### Burstiness: How Much Does Your Writing Vary?

Burstiness measures variation in sentence length and complexity. AI text tends to be eerily uniform — sentences hover around the same length, paragraphs follow the same structure, complexity stays flat. Human writing is messy. Short sentences. Then a long, winding one that piles clause on top of clause until it almost loses the thread. Then another short one.

Look at this AI-typical paragraph:

*"Machine learning algorithms can process large datasets efficiently. These algorithms identify patterns that would be difficult for humans to detect. The results can then be used to make informed decisions about various business processes."*

Every sentence is 10-14 words. Same structure. Same rhythm. Now look at a human version:

*"ML algorithms chew through massive datasets — fast. They spot patterns we'd never catch. And that's where it gets interesting: businesses can actually use those patterns to make calls they'd never have the data for otherwise, which completely changes the game."*

Wild variation. A 7-word sentence, then 7, then 35. That's burstiness. Detectors love seeing it.

## Category 1: Sentence Structure Patterns

These six patterns focus on how your sentences are built. Structure is the easiest thing for detectors to measure and one of the strongest signals of AI text patterns.

### 1. Uniform Sentence Length

**What detectors look for:** A standard deviation in sentence word count that's too low. If your sentences cluster between 14 and 18 words, that's a red flag.

**Example:** "The project was completed on time. The team worked efficiently throughout. The results exceeded initial expectations." — All roughly the same length.

**Fix:** Deliberately mix it up. Throw in a 3-word sentence. Follow it with a 30-word monster. Let your writing breathe unevenly.

### 2. No Sentence Fragments

**What detectors look for:** Every sentence being grammatically complete. Humans use fragments constantly in informal and even semi-formal writing.

**Example:** AI never writes "Not even close." or "Big mistake." as standalone sentences.

**Fix:** Drop in fragments where they'd feel natural. After a question. Before a pivot. Like this.

### 3. Absence of Run-On Sentences

**What detectors look for:** Perfect sentence boundaries everywhere. No comma splices, no sentences that probably should've been two.

**Example:** Humans write things like "I tried the new tool it was actually pretty good" — technically wrong, but natural.

**Fix:** Let a comma splice slip in occasionally. Don't make every sentence a grammar textbook example.

### 4. Formulaic Paragraph Openers

**What detectors look for:** Patterns like "Furthermore," "Additionally," "Moreover," "It is important to note that" starting every paragraph.

**Example:** "Furthermore, the data suggests..." / "Additionally, researchers have found..." / "Moreover, it is worth noting..."

**Fix:** Start paragraphs with "Look," or "Here's the thing:" or "So" or just... dive in without a transition word.

### 5. Perfect Parallel Structure

**What detectors look for:** Lists and multi-part sentences where every element follows the exact same grammatical template.

**Example:** "The system improves efficiency, enhances productivity, and increases output." — Three identical verb-noun structures.

**Fix:** Break the pattern. "The system makes things faster, your team gets more done, and output goes through the roof."

### 6. Consistent Sentence Complexity

**What detectors look for:** A steady level of syntactic complexity — no sudden shifts between simple and compound-complex sentences.

**Fix:** Alternate deliberately. Simple sentence. Then build a complex one with multiple subordinate clauses that winds through an idea before arriving at the point. Then go simple again.

## Category 2: Vocabulary Patterns

Word choice is where AI text really gives itself away. If you've ever wondered what does AI writing look like at the word level, this category is your answer.

### 7. Overuse of "AI Vocabulary"

**What detectors look for:** Words that LLMs lean on heavily: "utilize," "leverage," "delve," "tapestry," "multifaceted," "landscape," "foster," "comprehensive," "facilitate."

**Example:** "We must leverage these insights to foster a comprehensive understanding of the multifaceted landscape."

**Fix:** Use normal words. "Use" instead of "utilize." "Dig into" instead of "delve." "Complicated" instead of "multifaceted." Write like you talk.

### 8. Lack of Slang or Informal Words

**What detectors look for:** An absence of colloquial language, casual phrasing, or anything that wouldn't appear in a formal report.

**Example:** AI writes "this is quite impressive." Humans write "this is seriously cool" or "this slaps."

**Fix:** Sprinkle in informal language where it fits your audience. Not everywhere — just enough to sound like a real person.

### 9. Uniform Formality Level

**What detectors look for:** A perfectly consistent register throughout the entire text. No shifts between formal and casual.

**Fix:** Let your formality fluctuate. Be precise and technical in one paragraph, then drop into casual mode for the next. That's how people actually write.

### 10. Excessive Hedging Words

**What detectors look for:** Dense clusters of "perhaps," "might," "could potentially," "it is possible that," "arguably." AI hedges constantly because it's trained to avoid strong claims.

**Example:** "It could potentially be argued that this might perhaps lead to improved outcomes."

**Fix:** Take a stance. "This leads to better outcomes." Be direct. You can hedge sometimes, but not every other sentence.

### 11. Missing Contractions

**What detectors look for:** "Do not" instead of "don't." "It is" instead of "it's." "They would" instead of "they'd." A low contraction rate is a strong AI signal.

**Fix:** Contract everything you naturally would in speech. "Don't," "can't," "it's," "they're," "wouldn't." If you'd say it contracted out loud, write it contracted.

### 12. Over-Precise Language

**What detectors look for:** Unnecessarily exact phrasing where approximate language would be more natural. AI tends toward precision even when it doesn't matter.

**Example:** "Approximately 73% of users reported satisfaction levels above the median threshold." vs. "Most users were happy with it."

**Fix:** Round numbers. Use "most" instead of exact percentages when precision isn't the point. Be vague where humans would be vague.

## Category 3: Style and Voice Patterns

This is where the question of what does AI writing look like gets really interesting. These patterns detect the absence of a human voice — the personality gap.

### 13. No Personal Anecdotes

**What detectors look for:** Complete absence of first-person experience. No "I once..." or "In my experience..." or references to personal situations.

**Fix:** Drop in a brief personal reference where relevant. "I've tested a dozen tools and most of them..." Even a small one shifts the signal.

### 14. Missing Opinion Statements

**What detectors look for:** Balanced, neutral language throughout. No strong "I think X is wrong" or "This is the best approach, hands down."

**Fix:** State opinions. Disagree with something. Recommend something. Have a take.

### 15. Passive Voice Overuse

**What detectors look for:** High ratio of passive constructions. "The data was analyzed" instead of "We analyzed the data." AI defaults to passive because it's safer.

**Fix:** Rewrite passives to active. "We ran the analysis" beats "The analysis was conducted" every time.

### 16. Absence of Humor or Sarcasm

**What detectors look for:** Zero tonal variation in the direction of humor, irony, or sarcasm. AI plays it straight — always.

**Fix:** A dry aside, a self-deprecating comment, or a bit of sarcasm goes a long way. You don't need to be a comedian. Just be human.

### 17. Consistent Emotional Tone

**What detectors look for:** Flat affect. No shifts between enthusiasm, frustration, skepticism, or excitement within the text.

**Fix:** Let your tone shift with the content. Be excited about something that's exciting. Be frustrated about something that's frustrating. React to your own material.

### 18. Missing Self-References

**What detectors look for:** No "I," "we," "my," or "our" — or extremely infrequent use. AI tends to write from a detached, omniscient perspective.

**Fix:** Use "I" and "we" where natural. Reference your own experience, your own team, your own opinion.

## Category 4: Content Patterns

The final six patterns look at higher-level content organization. These are subtler, but sophisticated detectors absolutely check for them.

### 19. Perfect Transitions Between Paragraphs

**What detectors look for:** Every paragraph flowing flawlessly into the next with a clear logical connector. Real writing sometimes just... jumps.

**Fix:** Let some transitions be abrupt. Not every paragraph needs to connect smoothly to the one before it.

### 20. Balanced "On One Hand / On the Other" Structure

**What detectors look for:** Perfectly balanced pros-and-cons framing where every positive is followed by a counterpoint of equal weight.

**Fix:** Be unbalanced. Spend three sentences on one side and one on the other. Or skip the counterpoint entirely when your position is clear.

### 21. Formulaic Conclusions

**What detectors look for:** "In conclusion," "To summarize," "In summary," "Overall," as paragraph openers in the final section.

**Fix:** Just end. Make your last point and stop. Or circle back to your opening. Don't announce that you're concluding.

### 22. No Tangential Asides

**What detectors look for:** Every sentence directly serving the main argument. No digressions, no side thoughts, no "by the way" moments.

**Fix:** Go on a brief tangent. Mention something tangentially related. Add a parenthetical thought (even if it's not strictly necessary). It's what real writers do.

### 23. Over-Explanation of Simple Concepts

**What detectors look for:** Spending 2-3 sentences explaining something that could be said in half a sentence. AI pads for thoroughness.

**Example:** "Artificial intelligence, which refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence, has become increasingly prevalent in recent years."

**Fix:** Trust your reader. If they know what AI is, don't define it. Move faster through familiar ground.

### 24. Lists That Are Too Clean

**What detectors look for:** Bulleted or numbered lists where every item is the same length, same structure, same depth.

**Fix:** Make your lists uneven. One item gets two sentences of explanation. Another gets four words. That's how people actually organize thoughts — imperfectly.

## Fixing Your Scores: Quick Tips by Category

You don't need to address all 24 patterns to drop below the detection threshold. Here's the cheat sheet.

### Structure Fixes

Vary everything. Throw in fragments. Let a run-on slide. Start a paragraph with "Look" instead of "Furthermore." Break rules on purpose — but make it look natural, not chaotic.

### Vocabulary Fixes

Ban the AI word list from your writing. Use "use" not "utilize." Add contractions everywhere. Drop in a casual phrase or two. Write closer to how you'd explain something to a smart friend over coffee.

### Style Fixes

Add yourself to the text. State an opinion. Reference a personal experience. Crack a dry joke. Shift your tone — be enthusiastic in one paragraph and measured in the next. Let the reader feel a human behind the words.

### Content Fixes

Be imperfect on purpose. Skip a transition. Go on a tangent. Don't balance every argument. Let your conclusion just... end. Make your lists messy. Trust your reader to follow you without being led by the hand through every logical step.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many patterns does HumanizeIt actually check?

The full engine analyzes 40 distinct patterns. This article covers the 24 that have the most impact on your detection score. The remaining 16 are more granular statistical measures — things like token probability distributions, n-gram frequency analysis, and entropy calculations.

### Can fixing just a few patterns make a difference?

Absolutely. In our testing, addressing the top 5 highest-scoring patterns in a given text typically drops the overall AI probability by 20-35 points. You don't need to fix everything. Fix the worst offenders first.

### Do different detectors weight patterns differently?

Yes. GPTZero leans heavily on perplexity and burstiness. Originality.ai puts more weight on vocabulary and sentence structure patterns. Turnitin focuses on longer-range content patterns. HumanizeIt's engine is calibrated to approximate the weighted average across major detectors.

### Should I fix all 24 patterns or focus on the worst ones?

Focus on the worst ones. Diminishing returns kick in fast. If your text scores high on 3-4 patterns and low on the rest, fixing those 3-4 will usually be enough to clear detection thresholds. HumanizeIt's category breakdown tells you exactly which ones to prioritize.

### How often are new patterns added?

We recalibrate the engine regularly as AI models evolve and detectors update their methods. New patterns get added when we identify signals that detectors have started weighting more heavily. The core 24 covered here have been stable and high-impact since we launched.
